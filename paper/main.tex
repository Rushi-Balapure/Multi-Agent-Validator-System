\begin{abstract}
We study an inference-time validator that checks scientific claims against
original citations (D0) and independently retrieved abstracts (D1). The
system stages a sealed evidence reader, a judge, a citation auditor, and a
reconciler, then maps four-way internal labels onto SciFact's SUPPORT /
REFUTE / NEI space. The preregistered primary comparison is false
endorsement of Validator V versus same-evidence baseline B2. This paper
reports a SciFact-native evaluation: we do not add human four-way gold or a
human study. Those remain future work. If V does not reduce false
endorsement relative to B2, we report that as a negative result on the same
tables.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models are increasingly used to write and check scientific
claims. A system that endorses a claim the evidence does not support is
more harmful than one that abstains. We ask whether a staged, evidence-first
validator reduces that false-endorsement risk compared with a
same-evidence baseline that already reads the original citations.

Research questions retained in this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item RQ1 (primary): Does V reduce false endorsement versus B2 on SciFact?
  \item RQ2 (descriptive only): How does V split predicted NEI into
        unaddressed versus underdetermined? SciFact gold cannot score this.
  \item RQ4 (secondary): How do local and API model families compare on
        cost and the same metrics?
\end{itemize}
RQ3 (human study) is deferred.

\section{Related work}
Fact verification corpora such as FEVER and SciFact supply claim--evidence
pairs with a three-way label space. LLM-as-judge work shows that a single
model can assign those labels, but it also hallucinates support. Citation
auditing and selective prediction (risk-coverage) are the closest
mechanisms to our fail-closed auditor and confidence thresholds. We do not
claim a reproduction of trained verifiers such as KGAT or ARISTO; named
baselines here are prompt adaptations.

\section{Method}
The validator is a staged pipeline, not a single prompt.

\paragraph{Reader.} A sealed, claim-blind reader sees a neutral question and
shuffled passages. It must not see the asserted answer or gold rationales.

\paragraph{Judge.} A claim-visible judge assigns a four-way label
(supported, contradicted, unaddressed, underdetermined) to one evidence
scope.

\paragraph{Citation auditor.} D0 original citations are audited for
adequacy. Structural defects and parse failures are fail-closed.

\paragraph{Reconciler.} D0 and D1 judgments are combined. Agreement copies
the shared four-way label. Conflict or a failed D0 audit is
underdetermined, then mapped to NEI.

\paragraph{Label mapping.} supported$\to$SUPPORT, contradicted$\to$REFUTE,
unaddressed and underdetermined$\to$NEI. The mapping is the only bridge
onto SciFact gold.

\section{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Data.} SciFact abstracts, locked corpus hash in
\texttt{configs/corpus/scifact.yaml}. Splits: calibration 100, development
709, held-out local eval 300 (public SciFact dev), official test 300
unlabeled. The n=300 development sample is a seeded draw
(\texttt{data/manifests/dev300\_seed42.json}); the held-out 300 is unused
until one frozen run.

\paragraph{Design.} Evidence access (D0 vs D1) $\times$ reading policy
(claim-visible vs neutral evidence-first). Cells: B1, B2, B3, V-D1, plus
full V, prior-knowledge B0, and oracle O. Matched-budget B2@$k$ uses a
token-matched self-consistency vote.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Paired cluster bootstrap, 2000 resamples, 95\%
percentile interval. Families are connected components over shared
\texttt{cited\_doc\_ids}. Holm adjustment is applied to secondary metrics
only. The primary metric is false endorsement on the gold non-support
denominator.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} No human annotation, no four-way gold, no
sentence-level official evidence score, no human study.

\section{Results}
On the frozen development 300 (gpt-6-luna), B2 scores micro-F1 $0.747$ and
false endorsement $0.011$ $[0.000, 0.031]$. V never false-endorses on the
$268$ paired execution-ok claims (FE $0.000$), but SUPPORT recall falls
from $0.530$ to $0.383$ and $32$ V rows fail closed. The paired FE
difference (V $-$ B2) is $-0.012$ $[-0.031, 0.000]$ and does not exclude
zero ($p=0.46$). Following the preregistered decision rule, we report a
null primary result: the staged validator does not significantly reduce
false endorsement relative to B2.

The claim-visible D0 judge (B1) is stronger on F1 ($0.840$) with FE
$0.049$. Prior-knowledge B0 has FE $0.448$. The gold-rationale oracle
reaches micro-F1 $0.857$. V-D1 (D1 arm only) has micro-F1 $0.743$ and FE
$0.019$. Of V's $225$ predicted NEI rows, $60$ are unaddressed and $165$
are underdetermined; SciFact gold cannot score that split.

Held-out $300$ (one frozen run, $219$ families, $262$ paired claims)
reproduces the same pattern: B2 micro-F1 $0.760$ and FE $0.013$
$[0.000, 0.033]$; V FE $0.000$ with micro-F1 $0.618$ and $38$ fail-closed
rows. The paired difference is $-0.013$ $[-0.033, 0.000]$ and still
includes zero ($p=0.44$).

\section{Analysis}
If both methods rarely predict SUPPORT, RQ1 cannot discriminate endorsement
errors and the interesting contrast is precision versus coverage
(risk-coverage). V's reason codes (D0/D1 conflict, fail-closed, citation
defects) are the descriptive RQ2 table.

\section{Limitations and future work}
SciFact NEI is not four-way gold. We do not recover unaddressed versus
underdetermined as scored classes. Sentence-level evidence selection is
unscored. The human study and agentic-annotation protocol remain future
work. API model access and cost limit the number of families we can
compare.

\section{Ethics}
The task is interpreting published abstracts, not giving medical advice.
Outputs are labels and rationales over a frozen corpus. The API key is
never logged or committed.
